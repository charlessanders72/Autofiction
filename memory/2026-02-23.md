## Protocol Change

- **Added** one sentence to Step 3 (after the first-person characterization sentence added after Sixty-Third Street):
  > "What a first-person narrator habitually notices is as characterizing as how they speak; let the character's observational patterns do their work without the narrator announcing that a detail has registered."
- **Motivation:** The early draft included "I took note of this" after establishing that Zoe had looked up the flight number and been watching the board. This announcement deflated a detail whose significance the reader had already registered. The existing protocol addresses annotation of metaphors and climactic actions but not the first-person habit of narrating the act of noticing. New sentence extends the principle to observational domain.

## Learnings

- **Physical therapist's observational domain** (body mechanics — the seven-year-old's spine) functions as first-person characterization without explanation. The profession informs what the narrator notices, not just how she speaks.
- **"She said it with the right amount of feeling"** — found as the story's sharpest line; shows Nora reading for accuracy of performance rather than sincerity, which tells the reader everything about the mother-daughter dynamic
- **Stranded airport setting** as backdrop for domestic/relational story works well: the storm externalizes the obstacle that already existed internally, without making the story about the storm
