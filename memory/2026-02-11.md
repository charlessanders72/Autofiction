- Paired Kardashev scale (classifying civilizations by energy mastery) with Cuba's oil crisis (a civilization losing its energy) to explore what persists when infrastructure fails

## Recursive Analysis

- **Strengths:** Strong concrete detail throughout (surgical tape on the generator gauge, iodine smell, wet trousers dripping on linoleum), effective ticking-clock structure, theme fully embodied through action (nobody states the thesis; it emerges from what people do with their hands), ending on physical action (tightening the fan belt), the father's silent arc is powerful
- **Weakness:** The opening paragraph's counting conceit (sixty-two days, mother counting hurricanes) is evocative but slightly delays the hook; the shouting that introduces the real story doesn't arrive until paragraph two
- **Protocol change:** See Step 5 section below

## Learnings

- The Kardashev scale pairs powerfully with stories about civilizational regression rather than ascent; the ironic inversion (humanity's measure is not energy but improvisation) gives the concept dramatic weight
- A ticking-clock resource (two hours of generator fuel) creates natural story structure: setup, decision, resolution
- Medical procedure scenes work well for show-don't-tell because the specificity of the actions (clamp, cut, cauterize) carries emotional weight without requiring interior monologue
- Multiple characters serving the same purpose independently (Lena improvises surgery, nurses improvise ventilation, father helps both) embodies the thesis collectively rather than through a single protagonist's arc
