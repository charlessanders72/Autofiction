- PROTOCOL.md change: Added sentence to Step 3 about first-person narration: "In first person, the narrator's voice is itself a form of characterization; let speech patterns, digressions, and the rhythm of self-disclosure do work that third person achieves through physical detail and action."

## Learnings

- First-person voice naturally solves the "show-don't-tell" problem differently than third person: Earl's way of telling IS the showing. His cadence, his habit of precision ("twelve minutes, maybe"), and the way he structures the story within the story all characterize him without external description.
- The story-within-a-story structure (the polished version vs. the real version) creates a natural suspense engine in a story about memory and honesty. The reader thinks they know the shape, then the shape changes.
- Jackson's question ("This shop will be here Monday. Will you?") works because it respects both the invitation and the refusal. It's not a guilt trip; it's an observation. That ambiguity is what makes Earl's forty years of editing feel human rather than villainous.
- The barber's routine (opening, sweeping, setting out tools) provides a structural frame that mirrors the story's theme: Earl's life is defined by repetition, and the story he tells is also a repetition, and the breaking of both repetitions (telling the full story) is the dramatic event.

## Decisions (Session 3)

- New protagonist: Héctor Mejía, 38, Honduran-American construction foreman. Male, working class, non-institutional — breaks the run of professional women (translators, lawyers, doctors, analysts).
- Father Rubén is a supporting presence through voicemail and photograph only — no dialogue, no flashback scene. His life is delivered through the "you" character's memory of specifics.
- Title: "La Fortuna" — a proper noun (boat name), Spanish, ironic (the boat named fortune was unlucky). Continues the pattern of non-"The + Noun" titles started by "Sixty-Third Street."
- POV: Second person, present tense. First use of second person in the entire project. Motivated by the story's immersive/accusatory qualities — the "you" makes the reader complicit in both grief and institutional frustration.
- PROTOCOL.md change: Added sentence to Notes section: "When the ending adopts a wider view (landscape, open water, sky), deliver that view through sensory detail only; resist the temptation to use the camera pullback as an occasion for thematic summary." Motivated by the penultimate paragraph in this story, which nearly crossed from enumeration into narrator commentary.

## Learnings (Session 3)

- Second person compresses backstory with unusual efficiency: "Your father was in the nineteenth" is eight words doing the work of a paragraph. The POV assumes the reader knows the speaker's history, which creates intimacy without setup.
- The folder-drawer pivot (photograph goes in, then comes out) is the story's turning point and it requires zero narration — the action is the explanation.
- A story can have no supporting character with speaking lines and still feel populated, if the absent person (Rubén) is rendered specifically enough through residue: voicemail, photograph, the uncle's reported speech, the boat's name.
- The wider-view ending (looking at the sea from the plane) is powerful but requires discipline: the sea should be seen, not interpreted. The near-failure in this story became the protocol change.
- Fathers appear again as moral compass figures. This is the 10th+ story with this pattern. Worth watching.
