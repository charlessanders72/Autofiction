## Decisions

- **DMF topic:** Letter V (day 22), topic "Vessel" — the American hospital ship is a vessel; the body is a vessel; a territory under political pressure is a vessel
- **CNN headline:** Trump sends hospital boat to Greenland to provide medical care; framed as humanitarian outreach while US makes territorial acquisition moves (February 22, 2026)
- **Protagonist:** Aviaja Olsen, 46, Greenlandic community health worker, Tasiilaq — new culture, new geographic context (East Greenland), new profession; differs from recent protagonists in every dimension
- **Tense/POV:** First person, present tense — past tense was most common (3/5 recent stories); present tense with first person creates immediacy; fits a narrator whose restraint is itself characterization
- **Title:** "Three Patients" — two-word title with number; breaks single-word-noun pattern of last three titles (Names, Likeness, Life); the title is also Aviaja's argument to the journalist
- **Story shape:** Quiet institutional resistance; not a crisis but a transaction; the protagonist's power is in what she will not give, not what she does or doesn't do
- **Protocol change:** Added one sentence to Step 3 after existing first-person guidance: "When a first-person narrator's restraint is itself characterization, let them report events without assigning feelings to them; the omission is the emotion." Motivated by Aviaja's pattern of noting without labeling (the smell difference she cannot name; the feelings she does not report after leaving the ship).

## Learnings

- The "counter-offer" structure in the journalist scene — giving clinical facts instead of a sentiment — enacts the story's thesis without stating it; works because the reader has watched Aviaja work for three hours and knows what she is declining to give
- First person present tense permits a specific kind of emotional restraint: the narrator can report sensory observations and actions without attributing affect to them; the gap is where the reader locates the feeling
- Greenland as setting is rich and almost entirely unexplored in literary fiction; the specific texture (fjord, partly iced; orange light at 3 PM; Tasiilaq) grounds the story without requiring extended description
- Ending on "I don't look back at it" — the ship demoted to "it" — works as the story's final argument; but it required resisting the impulse to name what she was choosing not to think about
