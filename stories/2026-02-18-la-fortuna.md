# La Fortuna

**Date:** 2026-02-18
**Prompt Source:** Daily Micro Fiction letter R (Remains) x CNN headline (U.S. military confirms 43rd strike on small vessel in Caribbean/Pacific; 145 people killed since September, no evidence of drug trafficking provided; CNN, February 18, 2026)
**Tense/POV:** Second person, present tense
**Word Count:** ~780 words
**Recursive Analysis:** (see Step 4 below)

---

## Outline

**Theme:**
*Thesis:* When power turns a person into a category, the act of love is to insist on the particular.

*Conflict tests it:* Héctor has spent fourteen weeks trying to have his father reclassified from "suspected narcotics vessel occupant" to what he was: a Honduran commercial fisherman named Rubén Mejía. The government will not change its record. Every official channel has returned the same language: "incident report reflects field assessment at time of engagement," "no further reclassification is warranted."

*Arc resolves it:* The night before flying to Honduras to see the grave for the first time, Héctor stops fighting the record and instead puts the photograph in his shirt pocket. His version of his father cannot be erased, only carried.

**Characters:**
- **Héctor Mejía, 38** — Construction foreman in Houston, Texas. Born in San Pedro Sula, Honduras; permanent US resident for sixteen years. Works twelve-hour days on commercial builds north of the city. His father Rubén (67) was a commercial fisherman out of La Ceiba for forty years. Rubén was killed in October 2025 when a U.S. military airstrike struck his fishing boat in the Caribbean. The DoD classified the vessel as a "suspected narcotics vessel" and provided no evidence. Héctor has spent fourteen weeks pursuing FOIA requests, writing letters to his congressman (two form letters back), calling the DOD public affairs line (referred to State, which referred him back to DOD). He flies tomorrow at 7 AM to see the grave.

**Setting/Atmosphere:**
Héctor's apartment, Houston, Texas. Late evening, February 18, 2026. Kitchen table, a folder of documents. Television on CNN in the next room. His carry-on bag, zipped, by the front door. The apartment smells of the dinner he cooked and did not finish.

**Plot:**
1. Héctor at the kitchen table. CNN in the next room announces the 43rd strike. He has been keeping a list. His father was in the 19th.
2. He opens the folder: the incident report (four sentences, no names), his father's last voicemail, the photograph of Rubén on the La Ceiba dock holding a red snapper, La Fortuna behind him.
3. He puts the folder in the kitchen drawer. The photograph goes in his shirt pocket. On the plane over the Caribbean, he presses his forehead to the window and looks at the water until the clouds take it.

## Story

You have been keeping a list.

The anchor's voice comes from the other room, steady and impersonal, the way it always is when the news is about water and distance: *The U.S. military confirmed a strike on a small vessel in the Caribbean Sea today, the forty-third such action since September. Officials say the boat was carrying narcotics. No evidence was provided.*

Forty-three. You add it to the notebook beside the folder. Your father was in the nineteenth.

The folder is manila, three inches thick now. It started as a slim thing, just the incident report the State Department forwarded in November, four sentences typed in a font that looked like it was generated by a machine that had never met anyone. *Incident report: October 14, 2025. Caribbean Sea, coordinates redacted. One vessel engaged and neutralized. Vessel assessed as suspected narcotics transport. No further action required.* Four sentences. No names.

You open the folder anyway.

The FOIA request sits on top, stamped *Received* but not *Fulfilled.* Below it, two form letters from Congressman Harrington's office, the second with a different staffer's name in the signature line, which means the first one didn't reach anyone who stayed. Below the letters, the DOD public affairs number with the note you wrote in the margin: *transferred to State, State transferred back.* Below that, a CNN article from November, mentioning the strike briefly before pivoting to a Senate vote.

You reach past all of it for the photograph.

Your father is standing on the dock at La Ceiba. He is sixty-seven in this photograph, but he does not look it; he is laughing at something off-camera, and the laugh has pulled his shoulders back and widened his chest, and he is holding a red snapper by the tail, holding it out toward whoever is taking the picture, showing it off the way a man shows off work he is proud of. Behind him, La Fortuna, her bow angled into the light, the name stenciled in white on blue paint that your uncle told you your father repainted every April, every year for forty years, because a boat that looks cared-for fishes better, he always said.

You set the photograph down and pick up your phone.

You have played the voicemail so many times the screen goes straight to it now.

*Hector. It's your father.* He always announced himself. Sixteen years in this country and he still announced himself on voicemail, as if you might not recognize his voice. *I'm calling about Sunday. Your cousin is coming for his birthday and your aunt wants to know if you can come. Also, the snapper have been moving east of the reef, the water is cleaner there, you can see the bottom in eight meters, it's been a good week. Call me. I want to hear about your projects.*

A pause.

*Your projects* was what he called the construction jobs, always. He liked that word.

*Come see me when you can.*

He left it on a Tuesday. The strike was that Thursday.

You set the phone on the table. The television in the other room has moved on to something else. The dinner you made is on the stove, cold; the rice has crusted at the edges. Your carry-on bag stands by the front door, zipped, the tag already filled out: *Héctor Mejía, Houston TX. Destination: La Ceiba, Honduras.*

You put the photograph back in the folder. Then you take it out again.

The folder goes in the kitchen drawer, under the takeout menus. The congressman's form letters stay in the drawer. The FOIA stamped *Received* stays in the drawer. The four sentences with no names stay in the drawer.

The photograph does not.

You fold it along the crease already there from your wallet, and slide it into the breast pocket of the shirt you laid out for tomorrow.

Your flight is at seven. It is not yet midnight. You will not sleep.

---

On the plane, somewhere over the Gulf, you press your forehead to the cold window. The water below is very blue from this height. The flight path takes you southeast, over the Caribbean; you follow it on the small screen in the seatback until the clouds thicken and the screen shows only blue.

You do not know the coordinates of the nineteenth strike. The DoD redacted them, and the FOIA request did not come back.

Your father knew this water. He knew which currents ran warm in October, knew the reef east of the shoals by the color of the surface above it, knew which morning light meant the snapper were moving. That knowledge is not in any file. It is not in four sentences typed in a government font. It is in the tilt of his shoulders in the photograph, in his voice saying *come see me when you can*, in the blue paint he repainted every April on a boat he named for luck.

Below, the water passes. You look until the cloud cover takes it.

## Step 4: Recursive Analysis

### Strengths

- **Second person works:** The "you" simultaneously delivers intimacy and accusation. Compressed backstory ("Your father was in the nineteenth") lands with weight that would require setup in third person. The POV collapses the gap between Héctor's consciousness and the reader's, which is the right move for a story about a loss the reader should feel rather than observe.
- **The "four sentences, no names" motif:** Repeating the phrase carries the theme without stating it. The reader understands what it means to have a death reduced to four sentences before any character says so.
- **The voicemail scene:** Specific and earned. "He always announced himself" characterizes the father through a habit; the snapper moving east of the reef gives him a profession and a knowledge; "*your projects*" gives him a relationship with his son. None of this is explained.
- **The folder-drawer pivot:** Done entirely through action. The photograph goes in, then comes out. No narration is needed.
- **Ending opens outward:** The final image (the water passing, the clouds taking it) resists the "protagonist alone in a solitary gesture" trap. The story ends on something vast, not something small.
- **Craft compliance:** No em dashes, no phone face-down, no fluorescent lights, no silence as climactic beat.

### Weaknesses

- **Borderline thematic summary:** The penultimate paragraph ("That knowledge is not in any file. It is not in four sentences... It is in the tilt of his shoulders...") approaches summary under cover of enumeration. It survives because each clause is anchored in a concrete particular, but the construction is structurally close to the narrator explaining what the story means. A revision that cut "That knowledge is not in any file" and began with "Not in four sentences typed in a government font" would be stronger.
- **"A boat he named for luck":** The final clause of that paragraph tips slightly aphoristic. It earns its place because "la fortuna" is the boat's actual name, but it risks sounding like the narrator is pointing.

### Protocol Change

**Observation:** When a story ends on a wider view (landscape, open water, sky), the camera pullback creates pressure to explain why the camera is pulling back. The most natural failure mode is filling the expanded frame with thematic summary rather than physical detail. The current protocol warns against theme-stating and against flashback framing language, but doesn't specifically address the wider-view ending as a vulnerable moment for over-explanation.

**Proposed change (ADD one sentence to the Notes section, after the sentence about endings):**

> When the ending adopts a wider view (landscape, open water, sky), deliver that view through sensory detail only; resist the temptation to use the camera pullback as an occasion for thematic summary.

**Rationale:** The penultimate paragraph in this story nearly crossed from concrete enumeration into narrator commentary. This sentence warns future stories to keep wider-view endings grounded in what is physically observed rather than what the narrator decides it means.
